What the LC-PolScope sees, and what it reports

This simulates our LC-PolScope as QPSC runs it: 549 nm light, the five liquid-crystal settings at a swing of 0.05 waves, the Hamamatsu camera at 141.5 ms, the background our rig measured on 2026-10-29, the same reconstruction the microscope server uses, and the same colors QPSC gives the orientation color image. Change anything on the left and every image redraws. Click an image to look at one pixel.

New to retardance and orientation? The concept page explains them first. For how each mistake looks on real frames, see making an image.

Specimen
Specimen retardance
Retardance is how far the specimen holds back light polarized along its fibers compared with light polarized across them, in nanometers. More collagen, thicker collagen, or better-lined-up collagen all give more. A stored retardance image keeps it as nm × 100.
Fiber direction
Measured counterclockwise from horizontal, as you see it on screen. The orientation image reports this angle, and the color image shows it as a color.
Exposure
One exposure for all five frames. 141.5 ms is the saved tissue setting; 50 ms is for the test target.
The optics
Glass under stress is slightly birefringent. The lenses sit in metal mounts, so the microscope adds a faint retardance of its own: almost none in the middle of the field and up to about 4.7 nm at the corners on our rig. This is the real pattern, measured from our background images.
A background is the five frames taken on empty glass, slightly out of focus. It records the optics’ pattern, and correction removes it from every image. Turn it off to see what it was doing.
Full brightness at
Display only, like Brightness & Contrast in QuPath. QPSC opens retardance at 20 nm; this page starts at 10 nm so faint signals show.

How the two retardances combine

Each retardance is drawn as an arrow at twice its angle. The reading is the sum of the arrows, so the optics add to a fiber running one way and cancel part of a fiber at right angles to it.

Things to try

  1. Typical collagen. With Collagen fibers at 2.5 nm, compare On the slide with the Retardance image: they agree. Now turn Background correction off. The corners brighten, the empty glass reads 2 to 5 nm, and the color image tints them, although nothing on the slide changed.
  2. Direction matters. Keep correction off, choose One fiber, and click the fiber near a corner. Turn Fiber direction: at one angle the fiber reads several nm too high, at right angles to that several nm too low. The arrows show why. Turn correction back on and the reading is right at every angle.
  3. Faint signals. Set the retardance to 1 nm, about the diffuse background of liver cells. With correction off, the optics are larger than the specimen in most of the field.
  4. Saturation. Choose Test target at 141.5 ms. The brightest frames turn red and the spokes read lower than 17 nm. Lower the exposure to 50 ms and they read correctly again.
  5. Display versus data. Change Full brightness at. The images get brighter or darker; the numbers in the readouts do not move.
