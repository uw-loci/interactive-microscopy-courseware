LC-PolScope · operator walkthrough

Running the LC-PolScope with QPSC

Follow these steps to go from a powered-off microscope to retardance and orientation images in a QuPath project. Making an image shows what each mistake does to the result, and Settings explains why each setting matters. Labels in this style match the software exactly, including its British spelling of “Colour”.

Software version. The steps follow the build on our microscope, which is newer than QPSC 0.13.0. The released 0.13.0 lacks several items shown here, including the Stitching queue window and the reminder before each run.

What you will do for each slide

Calibration and background both include the slide's own glass, coverslip and mountant, so you repeat them for every slide. A run with one slide follows this order: focus, calibrate on a clear area, apply, collect the background, focus again, acquire.

1. Start the hardware

The lamp, filter, objective and Kohler alignment are all set by hand. The software cannot change or detect them.

  1. Turn on the lamp. Micro-Manager has no control over it.
  2. Check that the 549 nm filter is in the lamp path. The liquid crystals and retardance values are only correct at this wavelength.
  3. Turn on the Meadowlark liquid crystal controller, the stage controller and the camera.
  4. Turn the nosepiece to the objective you will use. QPSC records your choice of objective but cannot move the nosepiece.
  5. Set the light path selector on the right side of the stand. Use position 2 for the camera and position 5 for the eyepieces. Positions 1 and 2 look alike, so check the number.

Confirm at the microscope: power-on order, how long the lamp and crystals need to warm up before calibrating, and which light path position serves each camera.

2. Start the software

Start the programs in this order: Micro-Manager, then the microscope server, then QuPath. The server cannot start without Micro-Manager, and QuPath needs the server.

2.1 Start Micro-Manager

  1. Open Micro-Manager and load the configuration for the camera you are using. For the Hamamatsu camera, use MM2-Flash4-20261021.cfg.
  2. Close OpenPolScope and recOrder if either is open. Only one program should drive the liquid crystals.
Confirm at the microscope: the Micro-Manager version to open, where its server setting for outside programs is found, and whether OpenPolScope or recOrder must be closed first.

2.2 Start the microscope server

  1. Double-click start_server.bat. A console window opens.
  2. Wait for Ready for connections... in that window. Leave the window open for the whole session.

2.3 Start QuPath and connect

  1. Open QuPath.
  2. Open Edit > Preferences, category QuPath SCope. Check that Microscope Config File is the file for your camera: config_LCPolScope_Hamamatsu.yml for the Hamamatsu or config_LCPolScope.yml for the Oryx.
  3. If you changed the file, click Switch now in the Switch Microscope dialog.
  4. Open Extensions > QuPath SCope > Utilities > Microscope Configuration > Communication Settings...
  5. Click Connect Now. The status changes to Status: Primary connected (auxiliary will connect on use).
  6. Click OK.
Each camera has its own configuration file. The Micro-Manager configuration and the QuPath file must describe the same camera. A calibration applied to one file does not reach the other. A mismatch usually appears later as Objective Pixel-Size Mismatch -- Workflow Cancelled.

3. Load the slide and find focus

The microscope is inverted, so the slide sits on the stage face down with the coverslip toward the objective. The label goes on your right as you face the microscope.

  1. Place the slide on the stage, coverslip down, with the label on your right.
  2. Open Extensions > QuPath SCope > Acquisition controls > Stage Map.
  3. Check Slide in holder:. Choose the placement that says the label is on your right, or Placement A (as scanned) if no macro image has been placed.
  4. Open Extensions > QuPath SCope > Acquisition controls > Live Viewer.
  5. Click Live: OFF so that it reads Live: ON. Stage movement stays disabled until live view is on.
  6. Open Stage Control, then the Navigate tab, and move to your sample.
  7. Focus on the sample.

A very dark live image can be normal. Near the extinction state, empty glass is close to black and only birefringent structure shows.

4. Check the camera settings

Retardance comes from brightness ratios between frames, so the camera has to be linear and fixed.

  1. Open Extensions > QuPath SCope > LC-PolScope > LC-PolScope Setup... One scrolling window lists the steps in order, with a Ready to acquire? box at the top.
  2. In Micro-Manager's Device Property Browser, set Gain Auto and Exposure Auto to Off, turn gamma off, and choose a 12-bit or 16-bit pixel format.
  3. Click Recheck. The Camera: line in the box updates. Any Fix line about the camera must be cleared before you calibrate.
Confirm at the microscope: what the Camera: line shows for the Hamamatsu, since it may not have the same property names as the Oryx.

5. Calibrate the liquid crystals

Calibration finds the crystal settings where the least light reaches the camera, then sets four swing states around that point. Run it on the slide you will image, on empty glass close to your region.

5.1 Run the calibration

  1. In the Live Viewer, move to a clear, empty area near your region. Anything birefringent in the field makes the result worse.
  2. In LC-PolScope Setup, click Calibrate Liquid Crystals...
  3. Fill in the dialog using the table below, then click OK.
FieldSet to
Output folder:Leave the default.
Search:single_pass. The other option takes roughly three times as many exposures.
Exposure (ms):The exposure you plan to acquire with. Use 50 for the birefringent test target. See 5.3 for other samples.
Black level:Leave the prefilled value. Do not clear the field.
Measure on:100, unless dust or debris near the edges needs excluding.

Swing, wavelength and scheme are shown in the dialog for checking. They come from the configuration file and should read 0.05 waves, 549 nm and 5-State.

The LC Calibration In Progress window stays open for a few minutes. Keep the field clear and do not touch the microscope.

Cancel in the progress window does not stop the calibration. The window closes, but the crystals keep moving until the search finishes. Wait for the result before starting anything else.

5.2 Read the result and apply it

The LC Calibration Complete window reports the extinction ratio in its header.

Extinction ratioHeader wordWhat to do
100 or abovegoodContinue. This microscope usually reaches about 270.
80 to under 100acceptableCheck that the field is clear and the optics are clean, then calibrate again if you can.
Under 80poorMove to a cleaner area and calibrate again.
AnyunreliableFix what the warnings name, then calibrate again. Do not apply it.
  1. Read the warnings in the Summary tab. A warning that a state would clip at the acquisition exposure means the exposure is too long.
  2. Click Apply to acquisition. Until you do, acquisition does not use this calibration.
  3. Check Exposure, all states (ms):. Type your exposure if a different value is shown. The value is written to the configuration file for all five states.
  4. Tick the Black level box if it appears, then click Apply.
  5. Click Collect background now... in the LC palette applied window, then continue at step 6.

5.3 Choosing the exposure

Empty glass is already bright in the swing states, and birefringent structure pushes one of them higher. Choose an exposure that leaves room for the brightest structure you will measure.

SampleExposure on this rigWhy
Thin tissue sections with faint collagen, mostly under 5 nmAbout 140 msLow noise, and swing states stay below saturation up to about 10 nm
Birefringent test target, or anything near 20 nm50 msSwing states stay below saturation up to about 40 nm

These numbers depend on lamp brightness. To measure your own slide:

  1. In the Live Viewer, open the Camera tab.
  2. Move to the brightest structure you intend to measure.
  3. Type the exposure in Exposure, all states (ms):
  4. Click Measure all states. A row that turns red and says clipped means the exposure is too long.
  5. Click Save to Profile, then Save, if you change the exposure. The value applies to every objective on this microscope. Collect a new background afterward.
No warning appears if tissue saturates during a run. The Ready to acquire? box does not check exposure against your sample, so Measure all states on the brightest structure is the check to make.

6. Collect the background

The background records the lens strain pattern and the slide's own retardance so they can be removed from every tile. Collect it right after calibrating, on the same slide, at the same exposure.

6.1 Make a background profile, once for each kind of slide

A background profile describes the setup on the stage: holder, slide type and mountant. The dialog will not collect without one.

  1. Open Extensions > QuPath SCope > Utilities > Image Quality > Background Profiles...
  2. Click New...
  3. Check the suggested values against what is on the stage, type a Name:, and add the mountant after medium: under Attributes:.
  4. Leave Make this the active background profile ticked and confirm.
  5. Click Close.

6.2 Collect

  1. Stay on or near the clear area. Check the live image for anything sharp.
  2. Turn the focus 10 to 20 um out of focus. QPSC does not move the focus for you. Defocusing keeps dust from printing onto every tile.
  3. Click OK in the LC-PolScope background prompt. If you did not arrive from the calibration, click Collect background... in LC-PolScope Setup first.
  4. Fill in the Background Collection dialog using the table below.
  5. Click Acquire Backgrounds. A Background Collection Complete notification appears when it finishes.
  6. Refocus on the sample.
FieldSet to
Modality:lcpolscope
Objective:The objective in use.
Detector:The camera in use.
Background profile:The profile for this slide.
Acquisition Profile:The matching profile, for example lcpolscope_20x.
Exposure Times (ms):Read only. All five states show the exposure you applied, with 10 frames averaged per state.
Each objective needs its own background. Repeat this step for each objective you will acquire with.

7. Set up and start the acquisition

Use Bounded Acquisition to image a region defined by stage coordinates, as described here. If you have an overview image with annotations, use Extensions > QuPath SCope > Acquisition controls > Acquire from Existing Image, which needs the image open and adds alignment steps.

  1. Open Extensions > QuPath SCope > Acquisition controls > Bounded Acquisition.
  2. Fill in the open sections using the table below.
  3. Open the MODALITY-SPECIFIC OPTIONS section. The section starts collapsed, and Ready to acquire? is inside it.
  4. Read every line of Ready to acquire? and clear each Fix line. The box never blocks the run, so reading it is up to you.
  5. Check the settings in the second table.
  6. Open the AUTOFOCUS section and check Autofocus:. The default suits tissue. Tick Sample is already in focus (skip initial focus search) only if you have just focused.
  7. Read ACQUISITION PREVIEW for the tile count, time and disk space, then click Start Acquisition.
  8. Read the LC-PolScope: this slide reminder, then click Acquire. The reminder appears before every run and lists the calibration and background in use. Pressing Enter cancels, because Cancel is the default button.

During the run

The Microscope Acquisition Progress window shows positions acquired and time remaining. Stay off the bench, since a bump between frames shows up as false retardance along edges.

  1. To stop, click Cancel Acquisition. The current tile finishes first, so the stop is not immediate.

8. Open and check the results

Stitching starts by itself and runs in the background. The Stitching queue window opens to show progress, and finished images are added to the project without being opened.

Project entryContentsUse it for
Sample_001.ome.tifThe five raw states as channelsChecking saturation and focus
Sample_retardance_001.ome.tifRetardance, stored as nm × 100. A pixel value of 500 is 5.00 nm.Measuring retardance
Sample_orientation_001.ome.tifSlow-axis angle, stored as degrees × 100, from 0 to 18000Measuring angle at full resolution
Sample_orientation_color_001.ome.tifColor image: hue for angle, brightness for retardanceViewing and figures. Do not measure on it.

File names can also include the objective or modality, depending on QuPath preferences.

  1. Open the retardance image and look at empty glass. Glass should be close to black, with no grid across the tiles.
  2. Open the color image and look at the tile edges. Fibers should continue across them.
  3. To try other colors, open the retardance or orientation image and choose Extensions > QuPath SCope > Utilities > Project Tools > Orientation Colour View... Choose a Colour map: and Full brightness at:, then click Show on viewer. Stored data do not change. Export colour image... writes a new image.
Colors and numbers use different angle zeros. The color image is registered to our PPM images (angle × -1, plus 4.5°), and the orientation image keeps the measured angle. Both are correct, but their angle values differ.

Raw tiles, the calibration and the background for each run are kept in the run's folder inside the project, under lcpolscope_20x_1/bounds/. Copies of the calibration and background are in _protected, which tile cleanup leaves in place.

9. Change to the next slide

  1. Load the new slide and focus (step 3).
  2. Calibrate on a clear area of the new slide (step 5).
  3. Collect the background on the new slide (step 6).
  4. Acquire (step 7). Keep the exposure, objective and display settings the same across slides you intend to compare.

The settings table lists how often each setting needs repeating.

10. Shut down

  1. Save the project and close QuPath.
  2. Click in the server window and press Ctrl+C. The window shows Server stopped.
  3. Close Micro-Manager.
  4. Turn off the lamp. A field left lit overnight dimmed the tissue under it, so never leave the lamp on a slide.
  5. Remove your slide.
Confirm at the microscope: the power-off order for the controllers and camera, and whether the stage keeps its coordinates after power-off. Saved alignments depend on the second answer.
